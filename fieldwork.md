**Age:** ~52  
**Years in business:** 19 years at the same location  
**Setup:** 40-seat mess, open 11 AM – 3 PM and 7 PM – 10 PM. Sells fixed thali (₹70 lunch, ₹60 dinner). No online presence, no UPI QR code (cash only). Wife handles cooking, Ramesh handles serving and counting. One hired helper on weekdays.

---

## What Ramesh Bhai Said

> **1. "Monday is always full. Everyone is bored of their weekend cooking."**  
He has noticed — without any data — that Monday lunch is consistently his busiest slot. He prepares 10–12 extra on Mondays as a rule of thumb. He's right, but he applies the same thumb to every Monday regardless of month-end vs. mid-month variation.

> **2. "Wednesday and Thursday I never know. Sometimes 50 people, sometimes 70. No logic."**  
Mid-week demand is his biggest source of anxiety. He said he sometimes calls a nearby auto-driver to roughly count how many office workers seem to be around that morning — an improvised, informal demand signal.

> **3. "Saturday after 1 PM I throw a lot. But I can't make less — what if someone comes?"**  
He admitted that Saturday afternoons are his biggest waste window. He prepares nearly as much as a weekday because he's afraid of turning customers away, but Saturday footfall drops sharply after noon.

---

## 1 Surprising Insight

> **Rainy days paradox + delivery app skew:**  
Ramesh Bhai said he has always over-prepared on rainy days — "log bahar nahi nikalte, toh ghar pe khate hain" (people don't step out, so they eat at home). But in the last ~8 months, he's noticed the opposite on some rainy evenings: **Swiggy/Zomato orders from nearby apartments actually spike**, and customers send family members to pick up tiffins. He doesn't understand why some rainy days are empty and others are weirdly busy — and he doesn't know that the variable is *whether his area appears on a delivery app aggregator on that day*. He is not listed on any app himself, but his neighbours who *are* listed divert or overflow orders to him informally. This informal delivery overflow is completely invisible to him as a demand signal.

## Observation Notes

- No notebook or logbook maintained. All "data" lives in Ramesh Bhai's head.
- Food left over at 3 PM is offered to two construction workers who eat outside. No formal donation channel.
- Gas cost (~₹900/month cylinder) is his biggest concern after raw vegetables. Reducing cook time = reducing waste = direct saving.
- He was enthusiastic about a simple tool: *"Agar phone mein kal kitna banana hai woh aaye, toh bahut acha hoga"* ("If a simple phone tool tells me how much to cook tomorrow, that would be wonderful").
